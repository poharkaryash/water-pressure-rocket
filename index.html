SEC02

The problem, and what inspired us

This project, “Water Pressure Rocket”, was performed for the deeper study and analysis of the thruster and the thrust of the rocket. A water pressure rocket demonstrates how compressed air pushes water out of a nozzle to create upward thrust through Newton’s laws of motion. It also illustrates core rocketry ideas like reaction mass, pressure differences, and flight stability.

Newton’s Laws of Motion

  • First Law (Inertia): The rocket stays at rest on the launcher until compressed air creates enough unbalanced force to push it upward.
  • Second Law (Force & Mass): Higher air pressure and heavier reaction mass (water) increase the force and acceleration of the rocket.
  • Third Law (Action & Reaction): Water shooting downward out of the nozzle (action) pushes the rocket upward (reaction).

Pressure & Reaction Mass

  • Gas Pressure vs. Liquid Mass: Air compresses inside the bottle to build pressure, while water acts as a dense reaction mass that creates stronger recoil than air alone.
  • Nozzle Acceleration: The narrow bottle opening speeds up the exiting water, boosting thrust.
  • Changing Mass: As water leaves the bottle, the rocket becomes lighter and accelerates faster until the water runs out.

Aerodynamics & Stability

  • Drag & Streamlining: A nose cone and smooth body reduce air resistance (drag) during flight.
  • Flight Stability: Fins help keep the rocket flying straight rather than tumbling.
THE QUESTION WE ARE ANSWERING

Can a rocket made from reused soft drink bottles, with three extra bottles storing compressed air, launch reliably and fly steadily?

SEC03

How it works

  1. Fill

    Water goes into the main 2-litre bottle.

  2. Pressurise

    Air is pumped in and stored in the five 1-litre bottles.

  3. Release

    The nozzle on the launch pad is released and the pressure pushes the water out.

  4. Lift off

    Water goes down fast, and the rocket goes up. Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

SAFETY

Wear eye protection, launch outdoors, keep everyone well back, and never pressurise the bottles beyond what they can safely hold.

SEC05

CAD models

The rocket and its launch pad were modelled in SOLIDWORKS before and alongside the build, to check sizes, spacing and how the parts fit together.

Upgraded rocket: six bottles NEW

The first rocket used four bottles. The upgraded version adds two more, for six in total: the 2-litre main bottle in the centre, three 1-litre bottles spaced evenly around it, and two 1-litre bottles stacked on top through couplings. Bent external tubes run from each side bottle up to the stacked bottles, and two triangular wrap bands hold the three side bottles tight against the main bottle. Six 3 mm fins keep it flying straight: a large fin on the outside of each base bottle and three smaller fins around the top bottle.

Launch pad

The launch pad is a single multi-body part: the board deck, four PVC pipe legs, two steel angle brackets and the hollow centre pipe that the rocket nozzle sits on. It is driven by named dimensions such as deck size, pipe diameters and leg spacing, and mirror features keep both brackets and all four legs symmetric, so changing one value updates the whole pad.

SIMULATOR · PHYSICS ESTIMATE, NOT MEASURED
